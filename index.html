    Overview

    Introduction

    Physical education is a central component of the curriculum and enhances school education by adding a physical and motor dimension. It supports the development of students by strengthening motor skills, body awareness, self-confidence, as well as social and emotional competencies. Students gain a wide range of movement experiences, explore various forms of physical activity and play, and are empowered to participate actively, fairly, and reflectively in sports and movement culture. Physical education not only focuses on performance development but also emphasizes the joy of movement, the promotion of teamwork, and respectful interaction.

    Competences

    Every movement involves both control and energy, working together to create coordinated actions. To support effective movement learning, it's essential to develop body awareness, coordination skills, and physical fitness. These foundational elements are integrated across all areas of the physical education curriculum. The following six competence areas highlight how these aspects are addressed and developed through physical activity:

    Running, Jumping, Throwing Moving on Gymnastic Equipment

    Performing and Dancing Playing Sliding, Rolling, Riding

    Cycles

    The school years in Switzerland are divided into 3 different cycles. The first cycle includes kindergarten till 2nd grade. The second cycle is from 3rd grade till 6th grade and the third cycle contains 7th, 8th and 9th grade. The curriculum in PE is also divided into these 3 cycles.

    Warning

    The teachers cycle and the curriculum cycle do not line up. A teacher is normally for 2 years with the class: first two years of kindergarten, 1st - 2nd grade, 3rd - 4th grade, 5th - 6th grade and 7th - 9th grade. Therefore, it is difficult to cover all the competences, and proper communication when handing over the class to the next teacher is key.
