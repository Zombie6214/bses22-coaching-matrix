<h1 style="font-weight: 500; color: var(--md-primary-fg-color)">Focus Area</h1>


The competence area “Playing” is further divided into Movement Games, Sport Games and Combat Games. Each of these areas includes several subcategories. I have chosen to focus on receiving and passing, which is part of the Sport Games category. I selected this focus because I believe it is important in many ways and frequently used in everyday life. It helps students not only to participate in games, but also supports their motor, cognitive, social, and emotional development. 

Focusing on receiving and passing will support my development as a coach because it challenges me to work with a wide range of abilities. Some students are still developing basic motor skills, while others are quite advanced. This forces me to adapt my teaching, try different strategies, and learn what works best. It helps me build key coaching skills like adapting, differentiating, planning, and making decisions in real time.

As I am currently teaching a 3rd grade PE class, I have chosen to focus specifically on the second cycle.
However, it is important not to look at one cycle in isolation. The other cycles must always be considered as well. In my case, the first cycle is especially relevant, as my 3rd grade class has just entered the second cycle. This means that some basic requirements from the first cycle still need to be addressed before moving forward.

!!! tip "Think outside the box"

    The various competence areas and their subcategories often share similarities and intersect with one another. These connections should be taken into account when planning and implementing lessons.

## Demands of different Cycles
- First Cycle:
    - Receive and pass objects
    - Receive and pass a ball or other game object while running
    - Keep the ball or game object in play in small group situations
- Second Cycle:
    - Receive and pass the ball or game object in small team games
    - Receive and pass the ball or game object in simplified team sports
- Third Cycle:
    - Receive and pass the ball or game object appropriately in game situations

## Activities for the demands
### First Cycle
Receive and pass objects:

- Groups of two, passing all different kind of objects, vary the distance between them, vary way how to pass (bounce off the floor, bounce of the wall, two hands, one hand, foot, sticks)
- Working alone with a ball (throwing in the air catching again, make it more difficult e.g clapping between, spin)

Receive and pass a ball or other game object while running:

- Pairs are running from one side of the hall to the other while passing an object (variation: bouncing off the floor, change speed of running, use two hands, one hand, foot, sticks)

Keep the ball or game object in play in small group situations:

- Passing in small groups with a certain goal (e.g. ball isn't allowed to touch the floor, passing 10 times without a misplaced pass)
- Rope ball (simplified volleyball where catching is allowed)
- Hoop touch down (simplified rugby, not allowed to walk with the ball, team tries to touch down the ball in the hoop, different varietions e.g. several hoops, different field sizes, different balls)

### Second Cycle

Receive and pass the ball or game object in small team games:

- Passing game (small teams, which team reaches first 10 passes without getting interupted from the other team)
- Monkey in the middle (kids learn to pass while getting some pressure)
- Relays
- Students are in a circle, two balls, pass to every second student, can one ball overtake the other
- Line ball (simplified rugby, touch down behind a certain line, only 3 steps with the ball)

Receive and pass the ball or game object in simplified team sports:

- Floorball (but with less rules)
- Bench Soccer 
- Dice Floorball or Soccer (the number rolled determines how many players enter the field)
- Simplified volleyball (use a bench instead of a net or a lower net, ball is allowed to bounce off the floor once)
- Basketball with adapted rules (e.g. kids aren't allowed to walk with the ball, lower the basket)
- Playing team sports but adapting rules, field size and group size

### Third Cycle
Receive and pass the ball or game object appropriately in game situations:

- Play team games with normal rules (e.g. floorball, soccer, volleyball, basketball, ultimate, baseball, touch rugby, badminton)
- Invent extra rules (e.g. number of passes required before scorring is allowed)

### General activities
Kids often first need to learn how to throw and how to catch before they can play games with receiving and passing. Throwing is also part of the competence "Running, Jumping, Throwing". As described earlier the competences often overlap. 
Activities that help the students to learn how to throw:

- Throwing tennis balls with guidance
- Throwing tennis balls with a target (e.g. through a hoop, in a box, throw done cones)
- Two teams are positioned on opposite sides of the hall. Three large balls are placed in the middle. Students throw tennis balls from their side, trying to hit the large balls and roll them over to the opponents' side.
- Dodgeball (students love this game, they need to throw precise and if they catch the ball they save themselves.)
- Brennball (simplified baseball), one team needs to throw and opposite team tries to catch to get some points as well

### Difficulties

**Difference in skill levels:**

A class normally has around 20 students. Skill levels as well as motivation levels often vary widely. The gap in skill levels often results in only the more skilled students throwing and catching during games, and they tend to pass mostly to other skilled students. As a result, lower-skilled students rarely receive the game object, which means they have fewer opportunities to improve their skills, which makes the gap even bigger. 

!!! tip

    Keep this in mind when planning activities and adapt them in a way that ensures all students are involved so that everyone receives and makes passes.

Some Strategies/Activities to address this:

- Group composition (e.g. pairs of a better skilled with a lower skilled)
- Dividing class (pay attention to not discriminate someone)
- Specific game rules (everyone from the team needs to have the game objects before scoring is allowed, girls score counts double)
- Relays

**Fear of balls:**

A lot of younger students are scared of balls. It is important to address this and help them overcome their fear. 

Some Strategies/Activities to address this:

- Using softballs (this shows them that it does not hurt)
- Building up self-confidence (helps them to catch balls)
- Encourage them from outside
- Starting with small games where they don't get hit
- Working/playing with balls often

### Techniques

In this section we briefly cover the techniques of receiving and passing.

**Receiving:**

- Body position:
    - Body position depends on passing style and game objects. But you should always be ready to receive the pass (e.g. hands out of pockets, looking where your teammate with the game object is)
    - Show and teach the kids the differences (e.g. floorball: holding stick in both hands and stay deep, soccer: standing on the leg that is not stopping the pass, dodgeball: having the hands ready in front of the body)
- Moving towards the pass:
    - To receive a pass it is important to move towards it and not stand still or go in another direction
    - There are exeptions e.g. if a pass is played into the run
- Getting open to provide a good passing option:
    - To receive a pass you need to be free and not marked by an opponent
    - Running a way from the opponents, trying to have a direct open line to teammate with the game object
    - Talking together and tell if you are ready for the pass or not

**Passing:**

- Body position:
    - Body position depends on passing style and game objects. But your head should be up and your should have some body tension (for some games more than others depending on how much body contact is allowed)
    - Show and teach the kids the differences (e.g. floorball: holding stick in both hands and stay deep, soccer: standing on the leg that is not kicking the ball and place the ball correctly, dodgeball: having the ball in one hand, throwing over the head)
- Deciding where to pass:
    - Keep your head up (look out for teammates and opponents)
    - Talking together
- Passing precise:
    - Look at the spot where you want to pass
    - Regulate the force you put into the pass


### Impact as a Teacher
- Praise the students (encourage lower skilled students)

- Demonstrations (show them how to do it, but also let them show it to each other)

- Giving a lot of feedback (group and individual)

- Influence the group dynamic (avoid making fun of each other, often change groups, help building a supportive and secure learning environment)

- Goal setting (In the 2nd cycle of PE, every student should be able to reach the goals, therefore, the goal setting process is very important. Maybe even include them in this process and use a self-assess approach)
